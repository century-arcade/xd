# Access to the corpus

*Draft, November 2026. Marked **[established]** where it describes what we
already do, **[proposed]** where it is new.*

Most of the puzzles in the corpus are copyrighted by their publishers and
constructors. We hold them for research, under the terms below, and we do not
publish puzzles that are still under copyright.
This document says what is available, to whom, and on what terms.

## What the corpus is for

The corpus exists so that crosswords can be studied: their language, their
history, their construction, their editing, and the people who made them.
**[established]** It is not a place to get puzzles to solve. Puzzles for
solving are available from their publishers, and we point people there.

## Forms of access

From most to least open:

1. **Public results.** Statistics, reports and analyses at
   [xd.saul.pw](https://xd.saul.pw), and search at
   [The Cross(word) Reference](https://crosswordreference.com), where
   puzzles are shown as references with their answers filled in. No request
   needed. Publications can ask to be left out of the site (see
   [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)).
   **[established]**
2. **Derived data.** Datasets built from the corpus that do not reproduce
   puzzles: clue and answer pairs, answer frequencies, grid statistics,
   publication and constructor metadata. Most of it is published openly
   (see [DATASETS.md](DATASETS.md)); other cuts on request, for a stated
   research purpose. **[proposed]** Which
   derived datasets can be public is decided case by case and recorded in
   [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).
3. **Samples.** A random or targeted sample of complete puzzles, for a purpose
   that needs whole puzzles but not all of them, such as evaluating a solver.
   **[established]**
4. **The full corpus.** Membership in the private repository, for research
   that needs it, and for contributors who are adding puzzles.
   **[established]**

## How to ask

Open an issue in this repository, or write to xd@centuryarcade.org if you would rather
not ask in public, saying who you are,
what you want to do, and which form of access you need. We answer within a
few weeks. We say yes to most research requests; the questions we ask are
whether the purpose is research, whether a less open form of access would do,
and whether the result could put puzzles back into circulation.

The maintainers decide together (see [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md)). Each
grant is recorded privately: who, when, what form of access, and for what
purpose. **[proposed]**

## Terms of use

By accepting access to the corpus or to derived data you agree:

- To use it for research, and not for republishing or for any product that
  delivers puzzles to solvers.
- Not to give it, or any substantial part of it, to anyone else. People who
  need it can ask us.
- To cite the corpus in anything you publish that uses it (see
  [README.md](README.md#citing)).
- To tell us what you find. We like to hear about it, and it helps us show
  the corpus is worth keeping.
- To delete your copy if we ask, for example after a rights-holder complaint.

Access is personal. A grant to one person or group does not extend to their
collaborators, employer or successors. **[established]**

## Publishers and constructors

If you publish or constructed puzzles that are in the corpus, we are glad to
hear from you. We will tell you what we hold from your publication, correct
any errors you find, and record the constructor's name as they wish it
recorded (see [EDITORIAL.md](EDITORIAL.md), "Headers").

If you want puzzles removed, say which and why, and we will remove them. The
removal is recorded (see [PRESERVATION.md](PRESERVATION.md), "Removing
puzzles"), and anyone with a copy is asked to delete them. **[proposed]**

## Contributors

Editors, constructors and publishers who want to give us puzzles should
read [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md); nothing there requires access to
the repository. Volunteers who convert and submit puzzles themselves get
access in order to do so, and the same terms apply to the rest of the
corpus. The import guide is in the private corpus repository.

## What we can and cannot promise

**Rights.** We do not own the puzzles. Copyright in them stays with the
publishers and constructors who hold it, and nothing here transfers,
licenses or weakens it. For most of the corpus we hold no licence. We keep
the puzzles privately for research, and publish only data that cannot
rebuild a puzzle, in the belief that both are lawful; no court has said so,
and we have not asked one. A rights holder who disagrees should tell us
rather than wonder: we remove puzzles on request (above), and we keep a
record of each publication we have told what we hold. Anyone who receives
data from us is responsible for their own use of it. Our terms say what we
ask of you; they are not advice about what the law allows. **[proposed]**

**Accuracy.** The puzzles were converted from many sources over many years:
publishers' files, solvers' downloads, scanned pages read by OCR, and
hand-typed transcriptions. Headers are normalized, some dates are inferred
from filenames or page order, and constructor names are recorded as we
found them until someone tells us otherwise. Errors exist. We fix the ones
we find and the ones reported to us, and every fix is a recorded change, but
the corpus is not the publisher's authoritative text and should not be cited
as if it were. Counts and statistics describe what we hold, not what was
published. **[proposed]**

**Data.** The corpus and the public datasets are offered as they are, without
warranty. They change between versions as errors are fixed and puzzles are
added or removed, and a dataset can be withdrawn if a rights question
requires it. Cite the version you used (see
[README.md](README.md#citing)). **[proposed]**
