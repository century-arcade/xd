# Copyright renewal check: New York Times 1942-1963 and Judge 1931-1939

- Question: were the copyrights in the New York Times issues of 1942 through 1963, and in Judge magazine's issues of 1931 through 1939, renewed? This decides which of the corpus's older puzzles can be listed as public domain in DATASETS.md tier (a).
- Checked by: Alex DeJarnatt (drafted with Claude, to be reviewed by the maintainers)
- Checked on: 2026-11-06
- Scope: the corpus's 5,267 New York Times puzzles (1942-1963) and 531 Judge puzzles (1924-1939). No puzzle files were read; this is a check of the copyright records only.
- This is a record of what the Copyright Office's published records show. It is not legal advice.

Background. US works published before 1931 are in the public domain as of 2026. A US work published from 1931 through 1963 kept its copyright only if the renewal was registered in the 28th year after publication; works from 1964 on were renewed automatically. So New York Times renewals for 1942-1963 would have been filed 1969-1991, and Judge renewals for 1931-1939 in 1958-1967. Renewals registered through 1977 are printed in the Catalog of Copyright Entries (CCE), Third Series, Part 2: Periodicals. Renewals from 1978 on are in the Copyright Office's online records (the Copyright Public Records System, CPRS, which replaced cocatalog.loc.gov).

## Summary

**New York Times: renewed.** The New York Times Company renewed the copyright in each daily and Sunday issue of the paper, as a whole, throughout 1942-1963. Every renewal is registered by "The New York Times Company (PCW)", that is, as proprietor of copyright in a composite work, the claim that covers the issue as a collective work rather than a single article. Each issue has its own renewal number. The CCE lists them issue by issue for 1942 (in the 1969 and 1970 volumes), 1946 (1974 volume) and January to August 1950 (1977 volume), and CPRS holds an individual renewal record for 96% of the days in 1951-1963 and for every day of September to December 1950. The few days without a record mostly fall in known New York newspaper strikes, when there was no paper to renew. The belief behind the `puzzles-pre1965` release on century-arcade/xd, that the Times did not renew, is contradicted by the records. The renewed terms run 95 years from publication: the 1942 issues until the end of 2037, the 1963 issues until the end of 2058.

**Judge: no renewal found.** No renewal of any Judge issue, or of any contribution to Judge, appears in the CCE periodicals renewal sections for 1958 and July 1959 through 1967. The January to June 1959 volume, which would hold renewals for issues published in roughly the first half of 1931, is not on the Internet Archive and was not checked directly, but John Mark Ockerbloom's compilation of periodical renewals, which covers every CCE volume for 1958-1977, also records no issue or contribution renewals for Judge. Judge puzzles published 1924-1930 are public domain because of their date; on these records, those published 1931-1939 are public domain because they were not renewed.

### Sources that do not apply

- Stanford Copyright Renewal Database (exhibits.stanford.edu/copyrightrenewals): books only, so not searched.
- NYPL's transcriptions of the CCE renewals: books only. Ockerbloom's compilation (above) is the corresponding resource for periodicals.
- cocatalog.loc.gov has been retired; its records are now searched through CPRS. The CPRS website shows a bot-verification page to automated browsers; the same records were read through its public search API.

## Limits of the check

- New York Times 1943-1945 and 1947-1948 were not sampled individually. 1942, 1946 and 1949-1950 were, and each shows the same day-by-day renewal by the same claimant. Ockerbloom also describes the renewals as continuous from 1931. A gap in those years is unlikely but has not been ruled out.
- The 1946 and 1949-1950 CCE entries were read from OCR text, not page images; the 1942 entries were checked on the page images.
- The CPRS counts come from title searches and depend on how each record's title is typed. Days without a record have not been checked one by one, and a missing day is not evidence of non-renewal. December 1956 is the only long run without records that is not explained by a strike or a typing variant; it was searched by date and not found.
- The Sunday crossword ran in the New York Times Magazine. The renewals checked here are for the whole Sunday issue. Ockerbloom also lists separate renewals of the Magazine, which were not checked.
- Renewing the issue as a collective work covers what the Times owned in it. Whether that reaches a particular freelance constructor's puzzle depends on what rights the Times held in that puzzle, which these records do not show. That question only matters for deciding whether a puzzle is still under copyright. It does not make any 1942-1963 Times puzzle public domain.
- For Judge, one half-year volume (January to June 1959) was not checked directly. A renewal could also have been filed under a different title, or as a contribution under an author's name rather than "Judge". Author names were not searched.

## Recommendation for DATASETS.md tier (a)

- **Judge, 1924-1930: state as public domain.** Published before 1931.
- **Judge, 1931-1939: state as public domain, citing this check.** The records show no renewal. If certainty about early 1931 is wanted before release, the remaining step is to look at the January to June 1959 CCE periodicals volume (on HathiTrust or in print) for "Judge".
- **New York Times, 1942-1963: keep gated.** The issues were renewed and remain under copyright, until 2037-2058.
- **Separately:** the `puzzles-pre1965` release on century-arcade/xd publishes NYT puzzles from 1942-1964 on the belief that the Times did not renew. These records show it did. The maintainers should decide what to do with that release.
