# Governance

*Draft, November 2026, for a corpus begun in 2016 (see [HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md)).*

## Maintainers

The xd Crossword Corpus is maintained by Saul Pwanson, who started it in 2016,
and Alex DeJarnatt. The maintainers do the work, decide the policies, review
contributions and grant access. The corpus is a project of Century Arcade,
Saul's organization for century-scale preservation of games and puzzles.

## How decisions are made

The two maintainers decide by agreeing. Most decisions are made in
conversation and recorded as a commit, a merge request, or a line in
[DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md). A decision that changes a policy document is
made by changing the document.

Rules in the policy and editorial documents carry one of three marks:

- **[established]**: written down and agreed; contributions are checked
  against it.
- **[practice]**: what merged work already does, written down so it can be
  confirmed or changed.
- **[proposed]**: under discussion, not yet a rule. New work should follow it
  where it can.

A rule becomes established when both maintainers agree and it is written. If
they disagree, it stays proposed. There is no casting vote and no deadline.

## Changing the rules

Anyone can propose a change by opening an issue or a pull request in this
repository. Contributors to the corpus can also raise questions on a merge
request or on Discord. Proposals are answered by the maintainers; accepted
ones are logged in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).

## Advisors

The maintainers consult a small number of advisors: people who bring a
perspective the maintainers do not have, such as a constructor or editor, a
researcher who uses the corpus, an archivist, or someone with experience of
rights and licensing. Advisors are asked for their views once a year, when the
policies and the preservation assessment are reviewed, and when a hard case
comes up. They do not vote and have no duties.

## Continuity

If one maintainer stops, the other continues alone, with full authority, and
may invite a new co-maintainer.

If both stop, the corpus is to pass to a successor custodian who has agreed in
advance to receive it. The successor gets the repository, the source archives,
and this governance, and may run the corpus as they see fit within the access
terms. *(No successor is yet named. Naming one is on the preservation plan.)*

Whoever maintains the corpus holds the credentials to its hosting and knows
where every copy is. The maintainers keep a private inventory of these for
each other.
