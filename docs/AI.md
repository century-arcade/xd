# AI and the corpus

*Draft, November 2026, for a corpus begun in 2016 (see [HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md)). Marks as in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).*

## What the corpus is

The corpus holds puzzles made by people, by named constructors where the
name is known, as the publisher meant them: obvious misprints corrected
and recorded, nothing deliberate changed. It does not collect puzzles
generated by a program or an AI with no one constructing or editing them
(see [COLLECTION.md](COLLECTION.md), "Made by a person"). It does not
generate puzzles. **[established]**

## How we use AI ourselves

OCR models read page scans of old puzzles. Language-model tools help us
maintain the corpus: converting and checking files, reviewing changes, and
drafting documents, including this one. They do not compose puzzles or
clues; where a puzzle has no key, the answers may be solved from the clues,
and the source is recorded in receipts. Nothing they produce enters the
corpus without a person reviewing the change before it is merged. **[practice]**

## Using the data to train models

The tiers are those of [DATASETS.md](DATASETS.md).

- **Public-domain puzzles.** Anyone may use them for anything, including
  training models. We cannot restrict them and do not pretend to.
- **Public derived data**, such as the clue and answer file. Our terms ask
  that it be used for research, analysis, tools and solving, not to build
  systems that generate crosswords or clues for publication, and that the
  corpus be cited. We attach these terms to the data as signals: fields on
  the Hugging Face dataset card, and machine-readable training opt-outs
  where the venue supports them. They are terms we ask people to honor, not
  locks. **[proposed]**
- **Gated samples and the full corpus.** The terms in
  [ACCESS.md](ACCESS.md) apply, which forbid using them to deliver puzzles
  to solvers. We add the same term: not to build systems that generate
  crosswords or clues for publication. **[proposed]**

The clue file has been public for years, and has already been used to
train crossword-solving models.

## Constructors and publishers

Removal on request, as described in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md),
applies here too. A constructor who does not want their clues in the
public derived data can ask to be left out of future snapshots, and we
will do so. **[proposed]**
